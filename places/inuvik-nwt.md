# Inuvik, Northwest Territories

![Our Lady of Victory Church in Inuvik](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Front%20view%20of%20Our%20Lady%20of%20Victory%20Church%2C%20Inuvik%2C%20NT.jpg?width=1200)

## why this is here

Because the northern-job rabbit hole suddenly produced a place where **six-figure-ish public-sector salaries, a real town, Arctic wilderness, and an explicit Northern Allowance all coexist**.

Inuvik feels far away because it is far away. It is also a regional centre of roughly 3,200 people with a hospital, schools, roads, flights, recreation facilities, and enough normal life to make the move imaginable.

## the wait-what moment

GNWT's 2026–27 Northern Allowance for Inuvik is **CAD $14,304/year** on top of salary for eligible employees.

Checked **2026-09-08**, GNWT's current Inuvik listings included examples such as:

- Airfield Maintenance Specialist: **$87k–$104k**
- Airport/Marine Electrician: **$98k–$117k**
- Project Technician: **$101k–$121k**
- Community Counsellor: **$106k–$126k**
- Regional management roles well above that

That is where the conversation changed from "remote northern curiosity" to **wait, people are being paid serious money to live here**.

## what it seems like

Inuvik is the largest Canadian community north of the Arctic Circle. GNWT describes more than **50 days of 24-hour summer sunlight**, followed later by a winter stretch where the sun disappears for about 30 days and the town literally celebrates its return.

There is a 24-hour summer golf course, ski trails, parks, a major recreation facility, a regional hospital, and one of the world's northernmost greenhouses.

And unlike the tiny fly-in communities, Inuvik is connected by road through the **Dempster Highway** as well as year-round air service.

This is Arctic life with actual town amenities.

## things worth doing

- Drive the Dempster Highway at least once.
- Experience midnight sun properly instead of treating it as a fact on a screen.
- Come back for winter darkness and aurora.
- Use Inuvik as the jumping-off point for Tuktoyaktuk and the Arctic Ocean.
- Ski, hike, and see what a 24-hour golf course looks like under midnight sun.
- Spend time learning about Gwich'in and Inuvialuit culture and the Beaufort Delta region.

## getting there

Toronto is possible, but this is where the North begins charging airfare seriously.

A flexible Skyscanner check for **October 2026** found Toronto–Inuvik round trips around **CAD $1,488–$1,517**, generally with connections.

That makes Toronto visits a planned expense instead of a casual weekend, although a $14k Northern Allowance changes the arithmetic considerably.

**Checked:** 2026-09-08

## the live-here thought experiment

For somebody earning around $100k–$125k before the allowance, the proposition is remarkably respectable: small-town commute, real professional work, Arctic landscape, and explicit compensation for the inconvenience of living this far north.

The obvious things to investigate before getting romantic about it are housing availability, food prices, winter darkness, and the cost of leaving town regularly.
