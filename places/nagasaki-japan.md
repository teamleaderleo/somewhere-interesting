# Nagasaki, Japan

## the wait-what moment

Nagasaki's modern identity comes from centuries of being unusually connected to the outside world.

Dejima preserves the memory of the artificial island through which Dutch trade continued during Japan's long period of restricted foreign contact. The city also has Oura Cathedral, Glover Garden and the former Glover Residence, part of the UNESCO-listed Sites of Japan's Meiji Industrial Revolution.

Then there is a completely different 20th-century historical layer around the Atomic Bomb Museum and Peace Park.

Few compact Japanese cities carry this many overlapping histories.

## what it seems like

A harbour city built vertically enough that views keep appearing between buildings.

Streetcars help. Hills slow things down. The food reflects the city's history too: **champon**, sara udon, castella, Chinese-influenced dishes and old port-city mixing.

The strong version of the trip accepts the complexity rather than turning Nagasaki into a single memorial visit.

## things worth doing

- Walk **Dejima** and the old foreign-settlement / port areas.
- Visit **Glover Garden** for harbour views and the Meiji industrial-history thread.
- See **Oura Cathedral**.
- Give the Atomic Bomb Museum and Peace Park enough time and attention.
- Go up **Mount Inasa** after dark if visibility is good.
- Consider **Hashima / Gunkanjima** only with a reputable operator and current sailing conditions.
- Eat champon and castella because port-city history should also taste like something.

## rough trip idea

**3 nights:** enough for the harbour/history core, one major museum block, food and a night view.

**4–5 nights:** add Gunkanjima or a wider Nagasaki Prefecture extension.
