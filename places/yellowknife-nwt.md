# Yellowknife, Northwest Territories

![Northern lights at Yellowknife](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Northern%20Lights%20at%20Yellowknife.jpg?width=1200)

## why this is here

Because Vancouver can reach Yellowknife directly, and this is one of the most aggressively aurora-oriented cities on earth.

Northwest Territories Tourism describes Yellowknife as sitting beneath the **aurora oval**, on the edge of Great Slave Lake, with two main aurora seasons: late-summer/fall and winter.

## the place

Yellowknife is a small northern capital attached to a gigantic lake and an even bigger sky.

The trip sells itself through contrast:

- normal-ish little city by day,
- frozen Great Slave Lake / dogsleds / ice roads in winter,
- absurd aurora overhead at night.

NWT Tourism promotes everything from heated aurora cabins and Indigenous-led storytelling to dogteam viewing trips and winter excursions onto the lake.

## things I would actually want to do

- Book three nights around aurora viewing rather than betting the trip on one evening.
- Go out with an Indigenous-led aurora operator and hear the stories attached to the sky.
- Walk Old Town and the Great Slave Lake shoreline.
- Experience the lake frozen in winter at least once.
- Eat local fish / Arctic char.
- Try dogsledding or a snowmobile excursion if the season fits.

## rough trip

**3–4 nights** is the clean aurora-first version.

Build the nights around sky viewing and keep daytime intentionally light. The whole point is staying up absurdly late staring upward.
