# Nantong, Jiangsu

![Nantong skyline along the Hao River](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/The%20skyline%20of%20Nantong%20City%20in%20July%2C%202022.jpg?width=1200)

## why this is here

Because family kept insisting that Nantong's fish and shellfish were genuinely elite, which sounded suspiciously like hometown propaganda until the food showed up and made a convincing argument for itself.

The interesting part is that the boast is broader than "good freshwater fish." Nantong has a whole **江海河** food identity: Yangtze delicacies, inland freshwater species, and Yellow Sea seafood all belong to the local table.

## the wait-what moment

Nantong's own city government describes its aquatic resources as **“江鲜、海鲜、河鲜汇于一地”** — Yangtze, marine, and freshwater delicacies gathered in one place.

That makes sense geographically. The municipality occupies the north bank of the lower Yangtze and extends to the Yellow Sea coast, while rivers and canals run through the inland plain. The result is less a single "fish town" than a place sitting across several aquatic food worlds at once.

One useful correction to the family shorthand: some of Nantong's most famous "fresh" aquatic foods are actually **marine or tidal-flat shellfish**, especially out in Rudong.

## the seafood flex: Rudong clams

The headline ingredient is **如东文蛤 (Rudong hard clam)**, traditionally nicknamed **天下第一鲜** — roughly, “the finest fresh delicacy under heaven.” The title is delightfully modest.

Rudong's official local-specialty record says the clams were historically prized as tribute food and that the county developed large-scale managed clam culture on its tidal flats beginning in the 1950s. Rudong clams received a Chinese geographical-indication certification mark in 2014.

This is still a living industry rather than a museum-food story. In March 2025, Nantong reported the spring clam harvest beginning in Binhai Village, where more than **70,000 mu** of tidal flat were being used for clam culture within that village alone.

Other Rudong coastal staples include **竹蛏** (razor clams), **梭子蟹** (swimming crab), yellow croaker, pomfret, mud snails and laver/seaweed.

## the river side

Historically, Nantong also had serious prestige around migratory Yangtze fish. Local food history singles out **刀鱼 / 刀鲚** (knife fish / Chinese tapertail anchovy), describing Nantong fish as especially rich because they had only recently entered the Yangtze from the sea and had spent relatively little energy on the upstream migration.

The broader old **长江三鲜** tradition centered on knife fish, shad and pufferfish. Nantong's officially named dishes still preserve that history: its city list of famous dishes includes **清蒸刀鱼** (steamed knife fish) and **白汁鮰鱼**, alongside shellfish-heavy dishes such as **天下第一鲜** and clam pastries.

Treat the legendary wild Yangtze fish as culinary history when planning a meal today. China's **ten-year Yangtze fishing ban began on January 1, 2021**, and commercial capture remains prohibited in the Yangtze main stem and other designated waters. Current policy also prohibits marketing illegal catches under claims such as “wild Yangtze fish.”

## what I would actually eat

- **文蛤** — start here; simple preparation makes the point.
- **竹蛏 / razor clams** — another very local tidal-flat flex.
- **梭子蟹 / swimming crab** when in season.
- A cleanly steamed or lightly braised local freshwater fish, especially **鳜鱼 / mandarin fish** if available.
- **文蛤饼 / clam cakes** if somebody is selling them fresh.
- Whatever the family says is unusually good *that week*. In a cuisine this dependent on local aquatic produce, seasonality is part of the fun.

The recurring idea is **鲜 (xiān)**: sweet-savory freshness, delicacy, and the taste of an ingredient that arrived at the kitchen in excellent condition. Nantong cooking has every geographic excuse to obsess over it.

## rough food rabbit hole

**Day 1 — Nantong proper:** eat Jiang-hai cuisine, compare a freshwater fish dish with shellfish, then walk the Hao River after dinner.

**Day 2 — Rudong direction:** make the coast and tidal flats part of the story. Find a reputable local seafood place, order clams plus whatever is seasonal, and understand why people here can spend an entire meal arguing about the meaning of 鲜.

If family is around, abandon the plan whenever an aunt, uncle or grandparent says they know a better place.
