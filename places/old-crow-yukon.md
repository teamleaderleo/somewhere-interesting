# Old Crow, Yukon

![Old Crow, Yukon](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Old%20Crow%2C%20Yukon1.jpg?width=1200)

## why this is here

Because Old Crow is the version of the North where the map finally gives up pretending roads are mandatory.

It is **Yukon's only community without road access**, sitting where the Crow and Porcupine Rivers meet on the Traditional Territory of the Vuntut Gwitchin First Nation.

## the wait-what moment

The employment rabbit hole revealed that northern compensation sometimes includes very explicit recognition of isolation.

For Yukon education employees, the current collective agreement gives Old Crow a **$8,610 annual community allowance** and also provides eligible full-time employees with a claim equivalent to **one economy return airfare from Old Crow to Whitehorse for the employee, spouse, and one child once per school year**.

That is a wonderfully concrete way of saying: yes, living here changes the logistics of your life, and the job package should acknowledge it.

## what it seems like

Old Crow is small, close-knit, and deeply connected to the land. Travel Yukon places it near the migration route of the Porcupine Caribou Herd and about 60 kilometres south of Vuntut National Park.

The lack of road access has helped preserve a distinct relationship between community, language, culture, and the surrounding land.

The place also sits in an area rich in Ice Age fossils, including evidence of mammoths, steppe bison, and Arctic camels.

This is less “remote small town” and more **a settlement inside a huge living landscape**.

## things worth doing

- Arrive by air and actually feel what roadless means.
- Spend time learning about Vuntut Gwitchin history and contemporary life before treating the place as scenery.
- See the Porcupine River and the surrounding wetlands.
- Learn about the Porcupine Caribou Herd and its importance to the region.
- Look into Vuntut National Park and other northern-Yukon wilderness trips with appropriate local guidance.
- Experience midnight sun or deep winter, because either would completely change the place.

## getting there

You fly. That is the whole point.

Old Crow has no road connection to the rest of Yukon. Whitehorse is the practical southern gateway, and airfare is significant enough that employment agreements explicitly account for it.

**Checked:** 2026-09-08

## the live-here thought experiment

This is the high-commitment option.

The appeal is enormous for somebody who genuinely wants isolation, community, Arctic landscapes, and a life organized around the land instead of urban convenience. The tradeoffs are equally real: flights, groceries, housing, healthcare access, and a social world measured in hundreds of people instead of millions.

That is exactly why it belongs here. Old Crow makes the question “where could you live?” suddenly much bigger.
