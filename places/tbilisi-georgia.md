## why this is here

Because Tbilisi looks like somebody layered **hot springs, balconies, fortresses, churches, Soviet-era scars, wine, underground clubs and mountain-country energy** into one city and then somehow made it reachable from Frankfurt for roughly a few hundred euros round-trip on the cheap dates.

This is one of those places where the first five facts make the sixth feel inevitable.

## the immediate reaction

The city is literally tied to **hot water**. Georgia's tourism authority explains that the name Tbilisi comes from the Georgian word *tbili* — “warm” — referring to the natural hot springs under the city.

The old bath district, **Abanotubani**, is still full of working sulfur baths. Their brick domes poke out of the ground; inside, you can rent public or private bathing rooms, soak in geothermal water and get the traditional **kisa** scrub.

Then outside the baths you are in Old Tbilisi: steep streets, old houses and balconies, Narikala above you, churches and mosques close together, restaurants, wine bars, and the Mtkvari river cutting through the city.

At night, Georgia's tourism authority describes everything from traditional polyphonic singing and jazz to a serious underground electronic scene.

This city appears to contain several incompatible trips at once.

## things I would actually want to do

- Rent a private room in one of the Abanotubani sulfur baths.
- Get the kisa scrub and emerge as a new organism.
- Walk Old Tbilisi with no efficient route whatsoever.
- Go up toward Narikala / the high viewpoints around the old city.
- Eat khinkali until locomotion becomes difficult.
- Drink Georgian wine somewhere underground.
- Hear traditional polyphonic singing.
- Go out late and investigate the electronic-music side of the city.
- Use Tbilisi as the beginning of a much larger Georgia problem involving mountains.

## the Frankfurt portal

Checked **2026-08-29**:

- Lufthansa's FRA → TBS route page advertised round trips from about **€291** on the cheapest future dates.
- September examples started around **€336 round-trip**.
- Same-day / near-term fares were much higher — around **€496** for Aug 29 — which is a useful reminder that spontaneous geography costs money.
- Lufthansa lists the route distance at roughly **2,934 km**.

So this one rewards planning more than Hurghada does, but the planned price is still deeply reasonable for how different the destination feels.

## rough trip idea

### four days

**Day 1:** Old Tbilisi, Abanotubani, sulfur bath, enormous Georgian dinner.

**Day 2:** hills / fortress / churches / city wandering, wine.

**Day 3:** day trip outside the city or commit to the nightlife experiment.

**Day 4:** slow café morning, one more bath, buy something edible to take home.

### dangerous extension

Add several days and start looking at **Kazbegi / the Caucasus**. This is probably how a city break turns into an entire Georgia trip.
