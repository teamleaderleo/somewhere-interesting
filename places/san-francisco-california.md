# San Francisco, California

## why this is here

Because a work trip dropped me into **SoMa**, and within about ten minutes San Francisco had revealed itself as a city where wildly distinct neighborhoods sit almost on top of each other while giant corporate names loom over the skyline like somebody sold naming rights to a comic-book metropolis.

**Oracle Park. Salesforce Tower. Chase Center.**

Salesforce Tower in particular is an enormous glowing obelisk called *Salesforce Tower*. Lex Luthor would consider the branding a little heavy-handed.

## the wait-what moment

**SoMa literally means South of Market.** It is the broad part of the city south of Market Street, historically tied to industry, warehouses, rail and waterfront work. The city now divides and plans it through several overlapping sub-areas, including Central SoMa, East SoMa and Rincon Hill.

The eastern side can go from polished towers and museums to **I-80 / Bay Bridge approaches** in a handful of blocks. A perfectly ordinary city walk can therefore include passing under an interstate before continuing toward the waterfront.

This is an excellent introduction to American urbanism.

## neighborhood sampler

San Francisco seems unusually good at making a 20-minute walk feel like somebody swapped the city around you.

- **The Mission** — murals, taquerias, bars, Dolores Park, Victorian houses and a lot of street life.
- **The Castro** — historic center of gay San Francisco; colorful, hilly and culturally enormous for its physical size.
- **Haight-Ashbury** — Summer of Love / 1960s counterculture history, vintage shops and old Victorians.
- **North Beach** — Italian-American history, Beat Generation lore, cafés, old bars and a slightly old-world feeling.
- **Chinatown** — dense streets and alleys, restaurants, markets, temples and deep history; directly beside North Beach.
- **Nob Hill** — grand hotels, cable cars, mansions and absurd hills. Railroad-baron energy.
- **Russian Hill** — bay views, beautiful residential streets and Lombard Street.
- **Pacific Heights** — mansions. Serious mansions.
- **The Marina** — waterfront, bars, boutiques and the Palace of Fine Arts nearby.
- **The Richmond** — residential west-side San Francisco north of Golden Gate Park; excellent food and access toward the Presidio / Lands End.
- **The Sunset** — huge western residential district running toward the Pacific, with rows of pastel houses and a calmer feel.
- **Noe Valley** — pretty, affluent, residential, cafés and families. Almost aggressively pleasant.
- **Bernal Heights** — hilltop homes, neighborhood bars and great views.
- **Dogpatch** — former industrial waterfront turned lofts, restaurants, galleries and newer development. Delightfully unserious name.
- **The Tenderloin** — dense central neighborhood with historic hotels, immigrant communities, nightlife and very visible street poverty / drug activity.

## if there are only a couple free evenings

My first-pass shortlist would be:

1. **Mission** — eat, wander Valencia / 24th, see murals, end up around Dolores Park.
2. **North Beach + Chinatown** — they touch, so this is one natural long wander rather than two separate outings.
3. **Castro or Haight-Ashbury** — pick according to mood, or get ambitious and keep walking.

The real appeal seems to be **crossing neighborhoods on foot** rather than treating the city as a checklist of landmarks.
