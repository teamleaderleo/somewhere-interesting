# Tashkent + Samarkand, Uzbekistan

![Registan, Samarkand](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Registan%20square2014.JPG?width=1200)

## why this is here

Because Shanghai now has a **nonstop flight to Tashkent**, and Uzbekistan's rail network turns that arrival into a very clean route toward Samarkand.

China Eastern launched PVG → TAS service on **March 30, 2026**, currently four round trips a week. The flight is roughly **eight hours** westbound in the active schedule.

Then the railway does something extremely persuasive: the current timetable includes a daily **08:00 Tashkent Central → 10:25 Samarkand** service.

So this is no longer “Central Asia someday.” It is a direct flight, one good night's sleep, and then a morning train into one of the great Silk Road cities.

## the wait-what moment

Samarkand's old city is UNESCO-listed as **Samarkand – Crossroad of Cultures**. The big names are absurdly strong: Registan, Bibi-Khanum Mosque, Shah-i-Zinda, Gur-e-Amir, Ulugh Beg's Observatory.

The city developed at a meeting point of cultures for more than two millennia and reached a spectacular architectural peak under the Timurids.

This is one of those rare places where the famous photograph undersells the feeling of standing there.

## what it seems like

Tashkent first: broad avenues, Soviet and modernist layers, metro stations, parks, markets, plov, a real capital that deserves some attention of its own.

Then Samarkand turns the visual dial violently upward: blue tile, enormous portals, courtyards, domes and the weight of Silk Road history everywhere.

The practical beauty is that the trip can stay linear and civilized. Fly in, spend a day in Tashkent, take the train, live in Samarkand for a few days, then decide whether Bukhara has become inevitable.

## things worth doing

- Give Tashkent one full day: metro, Chorsu, plov, modernist / Soviet-era architecture.
- Take the train to Samarkand in daylight.
- See Registan once in bright daylight and again after dark.
- Walk Shah-i-Zinda slowly; this is not a “tick the box and leave” place.
- See Gur-e-Amir and Ulugh Beg's Observatory for the Timurid / astronomy thread.
- Add Bukhara if the trip grows to a week.

## getting there

**Checked: 2026-09-04**

- PVG → TAS nonstop: roughly **7h55–8h10** in current schedules.
- Current China Eastern schedule: **4 flights/week**.
- Official Uzbek Railways timetable currently lists **Tashkent Central 08:00 → Samarkand 10:25 daily** on train 768F.

This is a longer flight than the repo's easy-weekend entries, but the payoff-per-connection is excellent: one direct flight from Shanghai reaches a completely different historical universe.

## rough trip idea

**5 days:** 1–2 days Tashkent, train to Samarkand, 2–3 days Samarkand, home.

**7–9 days:** Tashkent → Samarkand → Bukhara. At that point the direct flight has become the opening move for a proper Silk Road trip.
