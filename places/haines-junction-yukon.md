# Haines Junction, Yukon

![Haines Junction](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:Redirect/file/Haines%20junction.jpg?width=1200)

## why this is here

Because this may be the most offensive combination yet of **respectable salary + tiny town + gigantic mountains immediately outside**.

Haines Junction sits beside Kluane National Park and Reserve and the Saint Elias Mountains, about 160 kilometres west of Whitehorse.

## the wait-what moment

Checked **2026-09-08**, Champagne & Aishihik First Nations had a Capital Projects Manager opening in Haines Junction at **CAD $104,097–$126,531**, with a robust benefits and pension package.

A separate Äshèyi Relations Manager posting listed the same salary band and benefits.

So this is not merely “beautiful place where maybe you can find seasonal work.” There are serious professional jobs here.

## what it seems like

Travel Yukon describes the approach perfectly: before you even reach town, the Saint Elias Mountains announce themselves.

Haines Junction is a small wilderness town at the gateway to Kluane National Park and Reserve. Pine Lake is less than 10 kilometres away, the Alaska and Haines Highways meet here, and the surrounding region is full of Dall sheep, mountain goats, moose, caribou, bears, glaciers, and some extremely unreasonable mountain scenery.

Parks Canada treats Haines Junction as the practical base for exploring Kluane's eastern edge. The deeper interior gets serious quickly: flightseeing, mountaineering, ski touring, expedition territory.

## things worth doing

- Use town as a base for Kluane National Park and Reserve.
- Drive the Haines and Alaska Highways with no urgency whatsoever.
- Hike in the Kluane region.
- See the Saint Elias Mountains from the ground, then from a flightseeing aircraft if budget allows.
- Swim or camp at Pine Lake in summer.
- Spend time at the Da Kų Cultural Centre and learn more about Champagne & Aishihik First Nations.
- Come back in winter because the entire visual world would be different.

## getting there

Haines Junction is about **160 km west of Whitehorse** on the Alaska Highway. Whitehorse has regular commercial air service, so the usual move is fly to Whitehorse and drive.

That makes Haines Junction remote without making it logistically absurd.

**Checked:** 2026-09-08

## the live-here thought experiment

This may be the cleanest “why am I fighting downtown Toronto?” entry so far.

You can plausibly have a six-figure professional job, benefits, pension, a tiny commute, and Kluane sitting beside town.

The obvious constraints are a very small social scene, limited services compared with a city, housing availability, winter, and the fact that the wilderness here is real enough to demand respect.

Still: **six figures with the Saint Elias Mountains outside is a legitimate argument.**
