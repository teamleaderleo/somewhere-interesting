## northern Ontario: tiny town + enormous outside

This category deserves its own branch of the project. Ontario gets dramatically emptier once you stop treating “north” as Barrie or Muskoka.

### Killarney

The polished entry point. The historic village sits at the end of Highway 637 on Georgian Bay, about 10 km from **Killarney Provincial Park**. The park packs pink granite coast, white quartzite La Cloche ridges and 50+ exceptionally clear lakes into a wilderness-class landscape.

The full La Cloche Silhouette Trail is major business; an easier future trip could simply be village + fish and chips + The Crack + one paddle.

Source: https://www.ontarioparks.ca/park/killarney

### Temagami

The canoe-country answer. More than 2,500 lakes, ancient red and white pine, and a huge interconnected paddling system. Ontario Parks describes a **2,400 km regional canoe network** across parks, conservation reserves and Crown land; the Obabika old-growth forest contains some of Ontario's largest and oldest white pine stands.

This is where “little town near the woods” becomes “town attached to weeks of possible canoe routes.”

Sources:
- https://blog.ontarioparks.ca/temagami-an-ancient-canoe-country/
- https://www.ontarioparks.ca/park/obabikariver

### Wawa + Lake Superior coast

Wawa works as a small-town hinge for one of Ontario's best pieces of road: the **230 km Lake Superior coast between Sault Ste. Marie and Wawa**. Lake Superior Provincial Park adds cliffs, beaches, waterfalls, inland lakes and both easy and remote shoreline.

This is the “Ontario somehow looks oceanic” trip.

Sources:
- https://www.ontarioparks.ca/park/lakesuperior
- https://northernontario.travel/sault-ste-marie/ontarios-best-coastal-drive-sault-ste-marie-wawa-along-lake-superior

### Marathon + Pukaskwa

This is where the Great Lakes start feeling like wilderness at continental scale. **Pukaskwa National Park** sits near Marathon; Parks Canada calls Thunder Bay the closest commercial airport at roughly 3.5 hours by road. The coast beyond Hattie Cove includes roadless, undeveloped shoreline, backcountry hiking and serious Lake Superior paddling.

Source: https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/on/pukaskwa/visit.aspx

### Terrace Bay + Slate Islands

A wonderfully weird one. The **Slate Islands** sit about 12 km offshore from Terrace Bay. Woodland caribou still roam the islands; there are no visitor facilities; the geology may be associated with an ancient impact feature. Getting out there turns a Highway 17 stop into an actual Lake Superior island expedition.

Source: https://www.ontarioparks.ca/park/slateislands

### Atikokan + Quetico

Atikokan is the gateway town; **Quetico Provincial Park** is the monster outside it: roughly 472,000 hectares of wilderness, more than 2,000 lakes, world-famous backcountry canoeing and International Dark Sky status. The Dawson Trail campground is about 45 km east of town, while the actual backcountry can swallow a week very easily.

Source: https://www.ontarioparks.ca/park/quetico

### Red Lake + Woodland Caribou

For the “okay, now we are genuinely out there” version. **Woodland Caribou Provincial Park** covers roughly 470,000 hectares and almost 2,000 km of maintained canoe routes, yet Ontario Parks says it welcomes fewer than 1,000 paddlers per season. It also forms part of the Pimachiowin Aki UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Source: https://www.ontarioparks.ca/park/Woodlandcaribou

### Moosonee + Moose Factory

Different category altogether, and one of Ontario's strangest reachable places. Take the **Polar Bear Express from Cochrane** to the end of the rail at Moosonee, then use local water taxis / guided experiences around Moose Factory and the Moose River estuary.

The communities sit in Moose Cree homelands near James Bay, with Cree culture and history, tidal water, migratory birds and a real possibility of seals or belugas on guided bay trips. Current summer 2026 Polar Bear Express schedule is five round trips per week, with the northbound trip about five hours.

Sources:
- https://northernontario.travel/northeastern-ontario/complete-guide-moosonee
- https://qr.ontarionorthland.ca/en/travel/service-updates

## northern Ontario shortlist by mood

**Want beautiful + comfortable-ish:** Killarney.

**Want classic Canadian canoe mythology:** Temagami / Quetico.

**Want an ocean-sized lake and tiny highway towns:** Wawa → Marathon → Terrace Bay.

**Want genuine backcountry scale:** Woodland Caribou.

**Want Ontario to stop resembling the Ontario in your head:** Moosonee + Moose Factory.

## current verdict

Toronto is useful at two opposite scales:

1. **Pearson can jump continents surprisingly quickly.**
2. **Stay inside Ontario and drive / train far enough north, and the province itself starts becoming alien.**
